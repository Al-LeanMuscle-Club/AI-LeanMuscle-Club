# Muscle under fat: three reflections on losing weight

**Author: [@AndysonYs](https://github.com/AndysonYs)** · Shared on October 27, 2026

I'm still a long way from a lean physique. My current goal is to have less fat covering the muscle, while keeping as much of my strength as possible during fat loss.

I started at a high body weight and went from 250 jin to 210 jin in about six months: from 125 kg to 105 kg, a loss of 20 kg. I can currently bench press 110 kg, deadlift 160 kg, and squat 120 kg.

There has been progress, but I have also taken some wrong turns. Here are three things that stood out to me, especially for anyone who, like me, enjoys food and needs to fit training around work.

## 1. Be careful with extreme food restriction

Early on, I tried losing weight by going without food or cutting my intake very sharply. The weight came off quickly, and at first I could clearly see that I was getting slimmer. It was easy to think I had found a particularly effective approach.

Over time, though, the visible improvements became less obvious, while the decline in my training was hard to miss. Weights I could previously press or squat became increasingly difficult. I felt that getting lighter had also come at a cost to my muscle and strength.

That made me reconsider what I wanted from training. How my physique looks and whether I have strength in the gym matter more to me than the number on the scale.

Food also gives me a lot of enjoyment. Being hungry for long periods was painful for me and noticeably affected my productivity and mood. Even if my weight was going down, an approach that left me without energy for work or training was difficult to sustain.

## 2. Doing a little still counts

Sometimes work leaves me tired, or I am simply having an off day, and I really do not feel like training. Even if I go, I might do only three exercises and fewer sets than usual. The session is nothing special.

But I have noticed that once I start, even an ordinary workout changes how I feel.

It gives me a clear signal: **I'm still keeping my fitness habit going today.**

Afterward, I am more willing to pay attention to what I eat. My sense of how the day went, and my satisfaction with it, improve too. The effects of an ordinary workout often carry into the rest of my day.

So on an off day, I let myself do less. A few exercises or fewer sets still deserve some credit. The positive feedback from keeping the habit matters to me; I do not need to feel especially pleased with every session for it to count.

## 3. When traveling, think about your “enjoyment-to-calorie ratio”

At home, my eating and training are fairly regular. But once I travel or go on holiday, it becomes much easier to let loose.

I really enjoy food, and there are always lots of things I want to try somewhere new. Suppressing that interest completely is difficult for me. One idea I like using as a reminder is the **enjoyment-to-calorie ratio: how much enjoyment am I getting from the calories I eat?**

If there is a local dish I have been looking forward to since before the trip, I will happily enjoy it. But I do not need to eat lots of ordinary hotel-breakfast food that I can get at home and do not particularly like, just because it is included or would otherwise go to waste.

The things I want to cut back on are those I was not looking forward to, did not find particularly satisfying, and ate simply because they were there. I think of those as my “low-value extras.”

This makes choices easier when I travel: eat regular meals, and save the extra appetite for food I am actually excited about. Before reaching for something, I ask myself: **Do I really want this, or am I eating it just because it is here?** As someone who enjoys food, I find that reminder useful.
